<Tip>
The **overall score** is a weighted average of the candidate's last two question sets (60% final set, 40% second-to-last). The six **skill scores** use every set the candidate attempted, weighted toward recent performance. The two can legitimately differ.
</Tip>

## The CEFR Scale

Equip's **English Communication test** grades candidates on the **CEFR scale**, the international standard for describing language ability. The candidate's overall score maps to a grade as follows:

| Overall Score | Grade | Label | What it means for hiring |
|---|---|---|---|
| 85 or higher | C2 | Near-Native | Understands virtually everything heard or read; handles any professional situation with the sophistication of an educated native speaker. |
| 76 to 84 | C1 | Advanced | Expresses ideas fluently and spontaneously; produces clear, well-structured texts on complex subjects. |
| 59 to 75 | B2 | Upper-Intermediate | Interacts fluently with native speakers; handles most business communication independently. |
| 43 to 58 | B1 | Intermediate | Manages standard workplace communication and routine meetings; produces simple connected texts. |
| 30 to 42 | A2 | Elementary | Handles simple, routine exchanges of information; needs support for complex tasks. |
| 21 to 29 | A1 | Beginner | Understands simple phrases; can interact if the other person speaks slowly and clearly. |
| Below 21 | &lt;A1 | Below Beginner | Little or no functional English; not yet ready for roles requiring English communication. |

## How Adaptive Scoring Works

The **CEFR Adaptive Test** does not show every candidate the same questions. It adjusts difficulty in real time to find each candidate's true level.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Candidate self-assesses">
    Based on a self-assessment question, the candidate starts at A1, B1, or C1.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Candidate answers a set of questions">
    Each **set** contains questions on Grammar, Vocabulary, Reading, and Writing. Listening and Speaking questions appear in alternate sets.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The level adjusts after each set">
    Strong performance moves the candidate up a level, weak performance moves them down, and reasonable performance keeps them at the same level.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The test ends when the level stabilizes">
    The test ends once the candidate stays on the same level for more than 2 sets, or after a maximum of 6 sets.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Overall score

The overall score uses only the **last two sets**: 60% weight on the final set and 40% on the second-to-last set. Earlier sets are excluded because the test was still searching for the candidate's level.

For example, a candidate who progresses A2, B1, B2, C1, C1 is scored only on the two C1 sets.

### Individual skill scores

Each of the six skills (Grammar, Vocabulary, Reading, Writing, Listening, Speaking) gets its own score. Unlike the overall score, skill scores use **every set** where that skill was tested, weighted toward recent sets.

For a five-set attempt, the weights are: set 1 (5%), set 2 (15%), set 3 (25%), set 4 (25%), set 5 (30%).

## Reading the Report

The candidate's report shows the overall score with its CEFR grade, plus a score and grade for each of the six skills. Here is a sample:

{/* screenshot: CEFR test report showing overall grade and six skill scores */}

| Measure | Score | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **Overall** | 72 | B2 |
| Grammar | 66 | B2 |
| Vocabulary | 66 | B2 |
| Reading | 66 | B2 |
| Listening | 66 | B2 |
| Writing | 51 | B1 |
| Speaking | 48 | B1 |

This candidate is B2 overall, with Writing and Speaking lagging slightly at B1, a common pattern.

<Note>
The overall score shows **where the candidate ended up**, while skill scores cover every set. A candidate who reached C2 in their final sets may still show B2 skill scores that include earlier, lower-level sets. Both numbers are accurate.
</Note>

<Tip>
Use the overall grade for shortlisting decisions, and the skill breakdown to probe specific gaps.
</Tip>
