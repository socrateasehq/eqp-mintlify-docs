title: "What Is the Trust Score?"
description: "The 0-100% score that summarizes how reliable a candidate's proctored session was."
---

<Tip>
The **Trust Score** is a 0 to 100% rating shown at the top of every candidate report. It summarizes how likely it is that the candidate attempted the test fairly. As a rule of thumb, review the full proctoring evidence for any score below about 80%.
</Tip>

Every completed attempt on Equip receives a Trust Score. Instead of asking you to sift through photos, screenshots, and audio clips for every candidate, the score condenses all proctoring signals into a single number so you can focus your review where it matters.

{/* screenshot: candidate report header showing the Trust Score at the top */}

## How the Trust Score Is Calculated

Equip's proctoring is powered by **AutoProctor**, and the Trust Score is computed automatically from the events recorded during the attempt. Violations detected by the active proctoring measures lower the score; a clean session keeps it high.

The signals that can generate violations depend on which [proctoring settings](/proctoring-settings) are enabled for each test. Typical examples include:

| Signal | Evidence captured |
|---|---|
| **Switched tabs or applications** | Event log, and a screenshot on supported browsers |
| **No face or multiple faces on camera** | Photos from the webcam |
| **Background noise or voices** | Audio recordings |
| **Leaving full screen** | Event log with timestamps |

Each test in an assessment gets its own Trust Score, and per-test scores are included when you [download results](/download-reports).

## Reading the Score

A higher score means fewer and less serious violations were recorded.

| Score | What it suggests | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Around 80% and above** | The session looks reliable | No action usually needed |
| **Below about 80%** | Enough violations to warrant a look | Review the full proctoring report |

<Warning>
A low score is a signal, not proof of cheating. Harmless events, such as background noise at home or an accidental tab switch, can register as violations. Always check the evidence before rejecting a candidate.
</Warning>

## Acting on Low Scores

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the candidate's report">
    From the Results page, open the candidate.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the Proctoring tab">
    Check whether the violations listed here look deliberate or incidental.
    {/* screenshot: Proctoring tab showing a violations list with timestamps and evidence thumbnails */}
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch the Session Recording">
    If [session recording](/session-recording) was enabled, play back the candidate's screen and actions to see exactly what happened.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Decide with full context">
    Weigh the evidence against the candidate's test performance.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
If many candidates score low on the same assessment, revisit its proctoring setup. For example, **Detect Audio** in a noisy environment or strict full-screen enforcement can produce frequent but harmless violations.
</Tip>
