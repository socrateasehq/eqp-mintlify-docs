Equip's **Communication Test** measures a candidate's English proficiency on the **CEFR scale**, the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages. It comes in two variants, **Fixed-Duration** and **Adaptive**, both fully auto-graded by AI and working on desktop and mobile.

Use it for any role where written or spoken English matters: support, sales, content, or client-facing engineering roles.

## Choosing a Variant

| | Fixed-Duration | Adaptive |
|---|---|---|
| Sets | 1 | 2 to 6 |
| Duration | ~15 minutes | ~45 minutes |
| Level | You choose a level, A1 to C2, when adding the test | Determined dynamically, starting at A1, B1, or C1 from a self-assessment |
| Best for | Checking candidates against one bar you already know | A precise read on the candidate's true level |

<Tip>
For hiring purposes, most recruiters require at least a **B2** level of English. If you already know the bar, the Fixed-Duration test checks candidates against it in about 15 minutes.
</Tip>

{/* screenshot: Add CEFR Test modal showing Fixed-Duration and Adaptive cards, with the level dropdown on Fixed-Duration */}

<Note>
Add this test by choosing the **English Communication** test type in the assessment builder, then **Add** on either card, picking a level first for Fixed-Duration. It is standardized content: you cannot handpick the questions, Equip randomizes them per candidate, and each question carries its own fixed time limit.
</Note>

## The Four Skills

The test evaluates the four core communication skills, Reading, Writing, Speaking, and Listening, through scenario-based questions. Every set contains 2 MCQs each on **Grammar** and **Vocabulary**, 1 **Reading** Comprehension, 1 **Listening** Comprehension, 1 **Speaking** Exercise, and 1 **Writing** Exercise, so the candidate's report shows **six individual skill scores**.

## How the Fixed-Duration Test Works

The recruiter picks a CEFR level when adding the test, and the candidate is scored directly against it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose a level">
    Pick the CEFR level to test against, from A1 (Beginner) to C2 (Near-Native), when adding the test.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Candidate answers one set">
    The candidate attempts a single set of questions at the chosen level.
  </Step>
  <Step title="AI scores the set">
    Equip's AI grades the responses directly, there is no self-assessment or level adjustment.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How the Adaptive Test Works

Instead of giving every candidate the same fixed paper, the test adjusts its difficulty as the candidate answers.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Candidate self-assesses">
    Before starting, the candidate rates their own English ability. Based on this, the test begins at A1, B1, or C1.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Candidate answers a question set">
    Each set covers Grammar, Vocabulary, Reading, and Writing, with Listening and Speaking appearing in alternate sets.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Difficulty adjusts">
    A strong set makes the next one a level harder, a weak set makes it a level easier, and a reasonable set keeps the same level.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The test ends">
    The test ends once the candidate has stayed on the same level for more than 2 sets, or after a maximum of 6 sets. Most candidates finish in 4 to 6 sets.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## CEFR Levels

Results are reported on the standard CEFR scale.

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Below A1 | Below Beginner |
| A1 | Beginner |
| A2 | Elementary |
| B1 | Intermediate |
| B2 | Upper-Intermediate |
| C1 | Advanced |
| C2 | Near-Native |

{/* screenshot: candidate CEFR report showing overall grade and six skill scores */}

<Tip>
For the **Adaptive** test, the overall grade is based mainly on the last two question sets, see [CEFR Scores](/cefr-scores) for the full breakdown. For the **Fixed-Duration** test, the grade reflects that single set directly.
</Tip>
